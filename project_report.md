# Project Report

## Project Title
**Think Before You Click: Phishing, Scams & Social Engineering**

**Prepared by:** Nikhil Jamkar

## 1. Introduction
Digital communication, online payments, social media, and cloud services are part of everyday life. Cybercriminals exploit these services through deceptive messages and psychological manipulation. This project presents practical cybersecurity awareness guidance to help people recognise suspicious activity and respond safely.

## 2. Problem Statement
People may trust unexpected messages, click unfamiliar links, download unsafe attachments, or disclose passwords and one-time passwords (OTPs) when pressured. Awareness and a simple verification routine can reduce these risks.

## 3. Objectives
- Explain phishing, online scams, and social engineering.
- Identify common warning signs in suspicious messages.
- Promote safe habits for links, attachments, passwords, and account access.
- Explain immediate response steps after a suspicious click or disclosure.
- Present the information in a clear, visually structured 10-slide PowerPoint.

## 4. Scope
The presentation focuses on general cybersecurity awareness for students and everyday internet users. It covers email, SMS, messaging apps, social media, online banking, and common impersonation scams. It does not perform technical analysis, scan devices, or replace professional incident-response support.

## 5. Tools and Technologies
- **Python:** Used to automate the slide creation.
- **python-pptx:** Python library used to create and format PowerPoint slides.
- **Microsoft PowerPoint or Google Slides:** Can be used to open and present the generated file.

## 6. Methodology
1. Select key awareness topics and arrange them in a logical sequence.
2. Write short, audience-friendly explanations and examples.
3. Apply a consistent dark-blue cybersecurity theme with contrasting accent colours.
4. Generate the title slide, topic slides, safety checklist, response guidance, and closing slide using Python.
5. Save the output as a `.pptx` file for review and presentation.

## 7. Main Content
The ten slides cover:
1. Title and presenter.
2. Why cybersecurity awareness matters.
3. Definition and purpose of phishing.
4. Common bank/KYC, prize, job, and investment scams.
5. Social engineering tactics such as urgency, authority, fear, and curiosity.
6. Warning signs of suspicious messages.
7. Five habits: pause, inspect, verify, protect, and report.
8. Account and device protection.
9. Steps to take after clicking or sharing information.
10. Key takeaways.

## 8. Expected Outcomes
After the presentation, the audience should be better able to recognise suspicious messages, verify requests through trusted channels, protect account credentials, and report suspected cybercrime promptly.

## 9. Limitations
The presentation provides general guidance and cannot guarantee that every scam will be detected. Attack methods change over time. Users should follow their organisation's procedures and consult official sources for current reporting information.

## 10. Conclusion
Thinking before clicking is a simple but effective cybersecurity habit. Checking the sender, verifying the destination, refusing to share confidential credentials, and reporting suspicious activity can help protect personal information, finances, and digital accounts.
